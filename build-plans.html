General notes

  • All dimensions in millimetres. Don't scale off the drawings; ask if a dimension is missing.
  • Check the setout and both diagonals (5 980) before digging.
  • Corner posts: 100×100 hardwood, in-ground durability class, cast straight into the footings, no brackets engineer. Ollie: a cast-in post must be H4 or an in-ground class hardwood.
  • All treated pine H3 above ground; stumps H4. Seal every cut end.
  • Bearers and joists run in one continuous length; no joins over supports engineer.
  • Bolts M12 hot-dip galvanised with 50×50 square washers under heads and nuts. Bolt counts marked "typical" weren't set by Ollie: two M12 per beam-to-post and bearer-to-post joint, staggered, 2.5 bolt diameters from edges.
  • Strap every rafter down to both beams (triple grip or cyclone strap each side).
  • Knee braces at all four corner posts engineer. Do a shake test when framed; if a post isn't stiff, add a second brace at the top corners engineer.
  • The laundry tower, outdoor kitchen and table in the model are by the owner, not in this scope.

Footings

Corner post footing · A1, A2, B1, B2
Stump pad · S1–S5

Corner posts engineer

  1. Dig 500 × 500, 800 deep, into the clay.
  2. Bitumen-paint the buried part of each post.
  3. Stand the post in the hole with its foot 150 off the bottom; plumb it both ways, check centres and diagonals, and prop it so it hangs there.
  4. Pour a 250 thick concrete pad: 150 under the post, post 100 into it. Don't take the post through the concrete.
  5. When it has set, backfill the remaining ≈550 with the dug soil, rammed in layers. The soil over the pad is what holds the post down in uplift.
  6. Leave the post tops long; cut them flush with the beam tops later.

Stumps engineer

  1. Dig 400 × 400, 200 deep.
  2. Hang each 90×90 H4 stump from its bearer, bitumen on the buried end, foot ≈100 off the bottom, and pour so it's ≈100 into the pad. (Replaces the anchors; with concrete under its stem.
  3. Line A and B stumps (S1, S2) carry the single bearer on top; line C stumps (S3–S5) sit between the two bearer plies.

Concrete

Corner pads 4 × 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.250.25 m³30 × 20 kg
Stump pads 5 × 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.20.16 m³20 × 20 kg

About 108 bags per m³, plus a few spare.

Deck framing plan

Footing and deck framing plan · looking down · studio at top

Bearers engineer

  • Lines A and B (the post lines): a single 190×45 H3 MGP10, 4.8 m continuous, bolted to the inside face of the posts, sitting on the mid stump.
  • Line C (middle, 2 000 out): a double 190×45 H3 MGP10, 4.8 m continuous, one ply each side of the three stumps, bolted through.
  • All bearers continuous and supported by an intermediate post or stump.

Joists and decking

  • 13 joists 140×45 H3, ≈3 810 in one length over all three bearers, ending flush with the outside of bearer B, plus a 140×45 edge board across their ends, at ≈445 centres, max 450 engineer. Ollie wants the bigger joists kept because the laundry and outdoor kitchen sit on this deck.
  • Joists set 20 in from each deck edge; edge joists stop at the posts.
  • Decking 90×22 H3, 41 rows of full 4.8 m lengths, ≈5 gaps, 20 overhang, notched round the studio-side posts. The deck stops at the shed-side posts, giving the shed steps more room.
  • Fix joists to bearers with triple grips (typical).

Corner bracing and bearer details

Corner post, bearer and knee brace · elevation along bearer line A (line B is the mirror)

Knee braces engineer

  • At all four corner posts: a timber brace at 45° from the bottom of the post up to the bearer, in line with the bearer.
  • Minimum 120×45; shown 140×45 H3, ≈465 long.
  • One M12 bolt each end. Bolt centres 200 above ground on the post and 430 on the bearer (on flat ground).
  • The brace runs on the inside face of the bearer, so a 45 packer on the post lines it up (my detail; Ollie only set the brace).
  • Shake test each post once framed. If it isn't stiff, add another brace at the top corners engineer.

Bearer fixings

  • Bearers A and B to each corner post: 2 × M12 through bearer and post (typical).
  • S1/S2: stump beside or notched under the single bearer (notch max half the stump), 2 × M12 × 180.
  • Bearer C: both plies bolted through each stump, 1–2 × M12 (typical).

Roof framing plan

Roof framing plan · looking down · studio at top

Beams engineer

  • B1 (studio side) and B2 (deck edge, shed side): each 2/240×45 H3 MGP12, one ply each side of the posts, 5 300 long from 6.0 m.
  • Beam tops level along their length; post tops cut flush with the beam tops.
  • Bolt each ply pair through the post: 2 × M12 per post (typical).

Rafters, approved as drawn

  • 6 × 190×45 H3 MGP10 at 930 centres, one over each post line and four between.
  • 3 760 back span between the beams, 1 630 cantilever toward the shed; 5° fall away from the studio.
  • Seat on both beams (small birdsmouth or packer for the 5°) and strap every rafter to every beam.

Fascia, purlins, sheets

  • Fascia 140×35 H3 across the rafter tips (approved).
  • 8 purlins 70×35 H3 on flat, full width, 300 past the end rafters.
  • Zincalume: 3 runs of 3.2 m sheets over the BBQ (fence) end, lapped 200 over a purlin.
  • Clear corrugated poly over the rest, lapped one corrugation over the Zincalume.
  • The roof drains onto the shed roof and into the existing shed gutter. No holes in the awning sheets.

Fence-end wall

Fence-end wall · from the deck side
  • Frame: 70×35 H3 studs at 600 between the post faces (≈3 660), bottom plate on the decking, top plate under the edge rafter, one row of noggings at ≈1 300. Every FC edge lands on timber.
  • Height: 2512 at the studio end (under B1), up to ≈2 750 under the rafter, 2196 at the shed end (under B2).
  • The wall is 82 thick (70 + 6 + 6) and sits inside the 100 posts.
  • Sheets: 6 mm FC both sides, 1 200 × 3 000 stood vertical, 4 per side, vertical joins on studs with a 2–3 gap.
  • Nails: galvanised FC nails ≈40 × 2.8: 200 at edges, 300 in the field, 12 in from edges, heads flush.
  • Finish: clear exterior FC sealer on every face and cut edge, ideally before fixing.
  • Optional: insulation batts in the cavity for a bit more sound reduction.
  • Text Ollie first: a solid wall is a bigger wind load on these two posts than the batten screen he checked.
  • FC is non-combustible, so the separate BBQ heat panel is no longer needed.

Build sequence and checks

  1. Setout (sheet 2). Peg post and stump centres; check 4 650, 3 760 and both diagonals 5 980.
  2. Dig the four corner holes 500 × 500 × 800 (A holes from the studio line outward) and five stump holes 400 × 400 × 200.
  3. Posts: bitumen the buried ends; hang each post plumb, foot 150 off the bottom, propped both ways. Re-check centres and diagonals.
  4. Pour the 250 corner pads (post 100 in). Stump pads later, with the stumps hung from the bearers.
  5. Hold point: let the concrete set before loading the posts.
  6. Backfill ≈550 of soil over each corner pad, rammed in layers.
  7. Stumps: bolt to the bearers, hang ≈100 off the hole bottom, pour the 400 × 400 × 200 pads.
  8. Bearers: single on A and B (inside face of posts), double on C; one length each, level, bolted.
  9. Knee braces at all four corners, M12 each end.
  10. Joists: 13 at ≈445 (max 450), one length, triple grips.
  11. Roof beams B1 and B2, 2/240×45 MGP12 each side of the posts; cut post tops flush.
  12. Shake test each post engineer. If it isn't stiff, add top corner braces before going on.
  13. Rafters 6 at 930, strapped to both beams; then fascia and purlins.
  14. Roof sheets: Zincalume at the BBQ end, clear poly over the rest.
  15. Decking, notched round the posts.
  16. Fence-end wall: frame, clad both sides in FC, clear seal.
  17. Seal all cut ends.
Engineer's changes in this set (Ollie Harris, 2 Nov 2026): single continuous bearers on A and B, double on C; joists continuous, max 450 centres, keep the bigger size; knee braces at all four corners, min 120×45, M12 each end; B1 and B2 2/240×45 MGP12; corner posts 100×100 in-ground hardwood cast straight in: 800 hole, 250 pad with 150 under the post, ≈550 soil backfill; stump pads 400 square × 200. Rafters and fascia approved as drawn.
