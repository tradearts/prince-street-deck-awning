Planting after review

What the local gardens doFig Landscapes in Bangalow, Streetscapes at Tuckombil and Cooke at Ewingsdale all use a short native palette in masses, grasses that hide the fence line, sandstone steppers and boulders on gravel, one or two sculptural plants, and swales that make the drainage part of the garden. This round applies all five.
Grass drifts on the fenceLomandra now runs in drifts along the base of the lane fence instead of single clumps, so the grey palings sink into green.
One focal plantA giant spear lily sits just inside the big gates, at the end of the view from the deck, with two palm lilies behind it for depth. The gates swing out and stay shut, so it has room for its 3 m spread.
Rain gardenThe shed downpipe at the lane end runs across the entry path in a shallow sandstone dry creek to a gravel basin under the blueberry ash, planted with Lomandra. The path steps over it.
Topper over the big gatesFrom the lane at 6′3″, planting alone couldn’t close the 1.8 m gap at the gates without filling the opening. A fixed 600 slat topper between the gate posts, matching the rest, does it; the gates still swing out underneath.
Two lush pocketsFollowing your reference photo, the shady studio corner and the south end of the shed–lane strip are now packed and layered: a tree fern canopy, broad-leaf palm lilies with red new growth, native ginger and cunjevoi for big leaves, a bird’s nest fern on a sandstone rock, and a glazed pot by the cafe window. All local natives standing in for the photo’s exotics. The sunny beds stay sparse so the pockets stand out.
Vertical screenThe fence-end screen now stands vertical: the same 70×45 battens on edge, 45 face and 45 gap, fixed to two horizontal rails on the outside of the posts toward the dividing fence. The bottom rail is screwed into the deck’s edge joist and the top rail hangs from the edge rafter at three points, so neither sags over the 3.9 m. The slats run down past the deck edge to hide the framing. One more 4.8 m length than before, about $32.
Shed wallA 400 mm bed now runs the full length of the shed’s clear wall, either side of the door and the rock steps: swamp lilies as the repeat, with native ginger and broad-leaf palm lily as accents. The candle vine is gone. Six copper rain chains hang from the shed gutter, which already takes the awning’s runoff, to a gravel dish at each group, so the plants under the awning get rainwater without any holes in the roof.
PaletteSixteen species and 61 plants: the lush species stay in the two shady pockets and along the shed wall, and the rest of the yard repeats Lomandra, Dianella and Callistemon. One Bangalow only: Byron’s planning rules say palms should be emergent, not the main feature.
